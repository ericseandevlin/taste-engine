# Memphis Revival — Validation Briefs (T1)

Six briefs across distinct domains, written to pull the Memphis/postmodern revival
vocabulary (clashing pastel + primary palette, squiggle motifs, scattered geometric
primitives, terrazzo/confetti speckle, black-white counterchange patterns, black
keylines, hard offset shadows, tilted elements, chunky display type, ornament that
does no functional work) while staying clear of the two named traps: Corporate
Memphis (flat bendy-limbed illustrated people, harmonized palette, no pattern
clash) and vaporwave (gradients, chrome, glow, scanlines, sunset palettes). Neutrals
must never cover the majority of any canvas.

---

## 1. Design fair — "Assembly '26" landing page

A three-day independent design and furniture fair held in a converted warehouse.
The page announces dates, venue, exhibitor list, and ticket tiers. Visual brief:
treat the page like a trade-show floor plan gone rogue — booth-card layout with
hard black keylines, a scattered field of triangles and crosses standing in for
"you are here" wayfinding marks, a terrazzo speckle band behind the hero headline,
and at least one wildly oversized primitive (a giant scalloped circle) looming
behind the exhibitor grid. Copy should sound like an actual fair: hall numbers,
opening hours, a founder quote about "objects with an attitude problem."

## 2. Kids workshop series — "Squiggle Club" after-school program

A neighborhood studio running weekly art and building workshops for ages 6-11.
The page sells parents on session themes (paper sculpture, big cardboard cities,
paint-splatter Fridays) and lists a sign-up form. Visual brief: lean into the
toy-block scale exaggeration and chunky rounded display type since the audience
is literally children, but keep every illustrated figure to abstract shapes only
— no cartoon kids, no bendy-limbed mascot characters, that would tip into
Corporate Memphis. Squiggles read as scribbles a kid might draw. Session cards
sit at small tilts like they were dropped on a table.

## 3. Creative agency — Studio Zigzag portfolio homepage

A five-person brand and motion design studio. The homepage has a manifesto-style
hero line, a strip of client logos, and three case-study teaser cards. Visual
brief: this is the most "grown-up" of the six but still commits fully — black
keyline cards with hard offset shadows in a clashing color, a checkerboard band
dividing hero from client strip, asymmetric placement so nothing centers, and
a rotated logotype in the nav. No gradients or glassy panels; every shadow is a
flat duplicate silhouette, not a blur.

## 4. Product landing page — Fizz sparkling water can launch

A single-SKU beverage brand launching a small-batch sparkling water line in three
flavors (yuzu, hibiscus, cucumber-mint). The page needs a hero can shot area,
a flavor-picker row, and a retailer list. Visual brief: the can itself (described,
not photographed — this is CSS/SVG only) sits on a terrazzo-chip pedestal, with
carbonation implied by a scatter of small circles fizzing upward around it rather
than any glow or bubble gradient. Flavor swatches are flat color chips with black
keylines, tilted at small alternating angles. Pastel base with one hard primary
red accent per flavor block.

## 5. Music festival — Loop City weekend festival page

A two-day outdoor electronic and indie festival. The page carries a lineup grid
by day, a stage map link, and a ticket CTA. Visual brief: the lineup grid is the
counterchange showcase — black-and-white stripe or checkerboard panels behind
alternating rows, with headliner names in chunky tilted display type sitting on
solid color plates. Zigzag "sound wave" motifs run as pure ornament along section
dividers, never as an actual waveform chart. Keep it explicitly not vaporwave: no
sunset gradient, no chrome lettering, no scanlines — everything flat and
hard-edged even though the subject matter (a festival) invites that cliche.

## 6. Stationery shop — Confetti & Co. paper goods shop page

A small shop selling greeting cards, wrapping paper, and desk accessories. The
page shows a product grid (cards, notepads, washi tape, desk trays) with prices
and an "add to bag" affordance. Visual brief: the product grid itself becomes the
terrazzo field — a confetti-chip background pattern runs behind the whole grid,
product cards float above it with black keylines and hard offset shadows, and
loose squiggle underlines mark section headers like handwriting flourishes. Price
tags are small tilted circle or starburst badges. This is the highest-density
ornament brief of the six; the products are real inventory, not illustrated
scenes, so nothing here reads as Corporate Memphis.
