# ITS Validation Briefs

Six briefs for synthetic International Typographic Style exemplars. Each targets a
different real-world content domain, written independently from the style doc's prose
(paraphrased, not quoted) and independently from any measurement code. Each brief states
the concrete visual moves the page must make and the specific traps it must dodge.

---

## 1. Chamber music season program page

**Domain:** A concert program page for a chamber-music series ("Autumn Sessions,
Season Eight") — the kind of printed handout patrons get at the door, listing the
five-concert lineup with dates, composers, and venues.

**Moves:** A strict repeating column armature carries everything: the season title,
the kicker line above it, each concert's date numeral, its composer/piece line, and its
venue line all snap to the same handful of vertical lines even though none of those
elements relate to each other in content. The season title is set enormous, four-plus
times the size of the running program copy, with almost nothing at intermediate sizes.
Composition is lopsided on purpose — a tall title block sits upper-left, a cluster of
flat rectangular bars (standing in for a sound/rhythm motif, not literal instrument
photos) sits off to the right at a different vertical position, and nothing mirrors
across the center. One warm red pulls out the date numerals and a couple of rules;
everything else is black, white, and grey flats. Thin hairline rules separate each
program entry, aligned to the same grid the text uses.

**Avoid:** No music-note clip art, no ornamental border around the program, no drop
cap on the title, nothing centered (not the masthead, not the venue block). The grid
has to be a real column structure that unrelated elements keep landing on — not just
"lots of whitespace and a sans-serif," which would read as generic flat minimalism
rather than this style.

## 2. Design department course index

**Domain:** A university graphic design department's course catalog page — a dense
listing of course codes, titles, credit loads, and instructor names for one term.

**Moves:** Every entry (course number, title, credits, instructor) is typeset in
columns that recur exactly down the page, so the reader's eye can drop straight down
any one of those vertical lines across a dozen unrelated course listings — this
recurrence is the whole point, not a decorative table with ruled boxes. Course numbers
are set in a jump-scale far bigger than the descriptive copy next to them, with weight
and position (not color-coding or badges) doing the work of showing which courses are
core versus elective. The page leans left and top-heavy, avoiding any centered
department masthead. A single blue marks section breaks and one rule under the term
header; the rest sits in black, white, and grey.

**Avoid:** No boxed/badge treatment on course numbers, no serif face pretending at
academic gravitas, no centered heading, no more than one type family. The alignment
between entries must be a literal shared-edge grid, not just consistent left-margin
paragraphs — otherwise it collapses into plain flat minimalism.

## 3. Civic transit service-change notice

**Domain:** A public transit authority's rider notice for a weekend service change —
the kind of bulletin posted in stations, in the Unimark/Vignelli identity-manual
tradition, explaining which lines are affected and what the alternate routing is.

**Moves:** A route diagram built entirely from flat circles, squares, and bars
(stations and line segments) sits on the same column grid as the explanatory text
block beside it, so the diagram's edges and the paragraph's edges land on shared
lines rather than floating independently. A single flat color band runs full-bleed
across the top edge of the page with no frame or margin around it, echoing the poster
habit the style inherited. Body copy is flush left, ragged right, no justification.
Hierarchy runs entirely through size, weight, and vertical position — the affected
line name is huge and high in the grid, the explanation is small and lower. One
saturated orange marks the alert band and the affected route; everything else is
neutral.

**Avoid:** No warning-triangle icon, no centered "ATTENTION" banner, no shadow or
bevel under the diagram, no hand-drawn arrows. It has to read as engineered signage
information, not a decorated flyer — persuasion by information, not by mood or alarm
graphics.

## 4. Photography exhibition wall text

**Domain:** A gallery didactic page for a documentary photography exhibition — the
title wall text plus a checklist of works, the kind of sheet mounted at a show's
entrance.

**Moves:** Dark ground — near-black background with reversed (white/light grey) type,
because the style isn't tied to one palette and this page should prove that. No
photographs are used (none are available); instead, flat rectangular fields stand in
for the prints themselves, placed asymmetrically on the grid at documentary,
matter-of-fact proportions rather than styled as framed art. The exhibition title is
set in lowercase display type, oversized against a small checklist of works below it,
each entry flush left with a hairline rule underneath, all snapping to the same grid
columns as the title block above. No accent color at all here — pure black, white,
and grey — to show the style can carry a page on structure and type alone.

**Avoid:** No vignette or gradient glow behind the title, no ornamental frame around
the flat rectangles standing in for photographs, no centered gallery-wall symmetry,
no painterly or textured treatment on the stand-in image blocks — they must stay flat
and unmodulated.

## 5. Structural engineering firm capabilities page

**Domain:** A one-page capabilities/portfolio sheet for a structural engineering
firm, the kind of page in a pitch deck or firm profile listing practice areas and
project statistics.

**Moves:** A diagram built only from hairline rules and rectangles abstracts a
structural frame (a truss-like grid of lines), placed off-center and sharing its
vertical edges with the text column beside it. Big stepped numerals report project
stats (years active, structures delivered, tallest span) at a size far beyond the
descriptive labels under them, with a repeating vertical spacing rhythm between each
stat block rather than ad hoc gaps. One green accent marks section numbers and a
single rule; the rest of the palette is black, white, and grey flats. The whole
composition is asymmetric — the stat column and the diagram block sit at different
heights and widths, never mirrored.

**Avoid:** No rendered/photographic building imagery, no centered firm logo lockup,
no embossed or beveled number badges, no drop shadows on the diagram lines. The
column grid has to be a real repeating structure the stats and the diagram both
align to, not just tidy spacing.

## 6. Design journal issue contents page

**Domain:** The masthead/contents/colophon page opening one issue of a small design
and typography journal — issue number, a short table of contents, and printer/credit
information.

**Moves:** No accent color anywhere — pure black, white, and grey, to show the style
holds up with zero chromatic help. The issue number is set in lowercase-adjacent,
enormous display type, radically larger than the contents listing beneath it, which
in turn is only slightly larger than the small colophon type at the foot of the page.
Masthead, issue number, and every contents entry share the same vertical alignment
lines even though they're unrelated blocks of content. Whitespace is treated as a
built part of the composition — large empty zones have edges that coincide with the
grid lines rather than being leftover gaps. Hairline rules are the only graphic
device separating sections.

**Avoid:** No spread symmetry (this is a single page, not a mirrored two-page
layout), no decorative drop cap on the oversized issue numeral despite its size, no
second typeface for "editorial flavor," no color introduced anywhere on the page.
