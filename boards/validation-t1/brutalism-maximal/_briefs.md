# Brutalism Maximal: Validation Briefs

Six self-authored briefs for synthetic HTML exemplars targeting the brutalism-maximal
strand (the anti-design lineage, not the raw-HTML minimal strand and not neubrutalism
UI-kit cuteness). Each brief names a real-feeling domain and a design mandate that
pushes toward harsh color clash, oversized cropped type, staged misalignment, and flat
zero-effects surfaces, while explicitly steering away from friendly pastel outline kits,
rounded corners, soft offset shadows, and any gradient or blur.

## 1. VOID CULT RECORDS: experimental/noise label

An independent label for abrasive electronic and noise artists. Audience: underground
listeners, cassette collectors, night people who already know what they are looking for.
The front page works as a release wall: type feels monumental and slightly violent,
cropped hard by the container edge like it barely fits the screen. Color clash stays
unresolved on purpose, acid green fighting pure red fighting cyan with no buffer tone
between them. Structural bones stay exposed: visible table rules for the release log,
plain native HTML buttons for player controls, zero icon dressing. This is not the
friendly neubrutalist look: no rounded pill buttons, no candy pastel, no cute
drop-shadow sticker cards. The mess reads as intentional because the grid underneath is
disciplined even where the surface collides.

## 2. AGGRO SEASON 04: streetwear capsule drop

A single-drop landing page for a small streetwear capsule. Audience: fashion-forward
younger buyers who follow drop culture. One oversized garment name crashes across the
viewport, cropped top and bottom by the browser frame. The product grid uses raw,
low-fidelity pixelated renders instead of retouched lookbook photography, shown at
native size with no crop and no decorative framing. Backgrounds are pure flat fields,
one panel pure black, the next pure acid yellow, no gradient wash between them. Buy
buttons look like plain native HTML buttons, not a soft floating pill; links keep their
underline and the stock blue is allowed to clash against the acid tones on purpose
instead of being restyled into a brand color.

## 3. NO FIXED ADDRESS: art collective manifesto and group show

A rotating art collective announcing an exhibition alongside its manifesto. Audience:
gallery-going, zine-reading art crowd. A dense manifesto text block runs edge to edge in
monospace, deliberately uncomfortable to read in one sitting. Member names and show
dates overlap each other through rotation transforms, sitting off-grid on purpose, but a
visible structural table underneath (exposed cell borders) proves the collision is
staged rather than broken. Artist bios sit inside hard black rules, never soft
box-shadow cards. Color plan: a pure red slab behind the manifesto, a cyan slab behind
the roster, nothing grey mediating between them.

## 4. OFFCUT: independent culture zine, web edition

A photocopied-feeling zine's web mirror of its print run. Audience: zine subscribers and
small-press readers. The masthead is monumental and cropped by the browser edge on
desktop. Body copy runs in Times beside a monospace sidebar, deliberately mismatched
rather than harmonized. The table of contents is a dense, utilitarian list with minimal
padding, but it sits inside a maximal color field rather than a plain white page, so it
earns the maximal strand instead of reading as accidentally unstyled. Pull quotes
overlap the article text by a fixed offset, unapologetically. No pastel highlight boxes,
no rounded chip tags for categories; categories stay plain bracketed text like
[ISSUE 04].

## 5. STATIC//NIGHTS: recurring club night series

A flyer site for a recurring club night. Audience: club kids, ravers, DJs booking talent
for future dates. The homepage is essentially one gigantic date and venue name, cropped
by the viewport so part of a digit falls off screen on purpose, forcing a sense of raw
scale. The lineup is a dense, utilitarian table with visible cell borders, never a card
treatment. The RSVP form uses completely unstyled native form widgets, sitting exposed
on a pure cyan field directly against a pure red block with a hard black seam between
them. No soft glow effects of the kind rave flyers usually reach for, no blur, no
gradient; everything stays flat and confrontational.

## 6. Self-promo portfolio for a graphic designer

An individual designer's self-authored portfolio, built to double as a stress test of
their own control. Audience: creative directors, art buyers, peers sizing up the work.
Project thumbnails sit deliberately misaligned across a visible grid, overlapping by a
fixed offset, while the footer credits block sits in a dense, utilitarian, correctly
boring table to prove the same designer can also do restraint when it counts. Typeface
mix is system Arial Black for name and nav, Times for project descriptions, monospace
for metadata (year, medium, client) sitting hard against a wall of pure yellow. No soft
hover states, no elegant fade transitions, no drop shadow on the thumbnails; the hover
state is a jarring hard color invert instead.
